# MIDI pipeline and LED rendering — November 14, 2026

## Conditions

Pi Zero 2 W Rev 1.0, Python 3.11.2, kernel `6.12.109+rpt-rpi-v7`, 195 LEDs.
The baseline runtime is `9aa05ea`; the candidate is `5d2972f`.
Both use the same instrumented benchmark and probe, identified by SHA-256 in
the [JSON data](pipeline_2026-11-14.json).

Virtual ALSA input, production pipeline, real GPIO/DMA, instrumented MIDI output
without a piano. Each chord contains twelve notes and pedal events. Counts and
hashes are compared at four boundaries: emission, reception, processing and
forwarding. No key, light or audio sensor is connected: these results are not
physical latency measurements.

The service was stopped during the tests and then restarted; its configuration
is unchanged. Preparation, warm-up and shutdown are included in the process
durations below. The chord period is a target: the generator can slow down under
load. It does not guarantee a rate of 1 300 or 5 200 events/s.

Every sequence is complete, in order, without duplicates and with identical
hashes. Latency figures are in milliseconds.

In the short comparison, the candidate's mean increases by approximately
0.317 ms. This cost accompanies frame arbitration and note protection; it is
not a demonstrated latency optimization. A single before/after pair cannot
distinguish this cost from all system variability.

For the extended test, the probe's latency samples cover the last 100 000
events; counts and hashes cover all 390 052 events.

The 81.214 ms frame spike remains in the results: its cause was not identified
during this acquisition. The load test therefore does not guarantee the absence
of spikes, even with zero events lost.

During the extended test, sampled RSS rises from 48 951 296 to 53 657 600 bytes.
Across the final fifty samples, it stays between 53 620 736 and 53 657 600 bytes.
This shows a plateau over this approximately seven-minute test, not proof of
stability over several hours.
The temperature reaches 48.3 °C; `throttled=0x0` before and after each test.

The queues are drained, no note or pedal remains active, and the buffer is black
after each shutdown. Raw instrumentation captures remain local; the published
JSON retains counts, hashes, durations, queues and RSS/CPU samples, without
private settings or network peer names.
